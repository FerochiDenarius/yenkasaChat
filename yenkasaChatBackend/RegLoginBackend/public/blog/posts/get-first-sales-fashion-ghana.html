How to Get Your First 10 Sales as a Fashion Seller in Ghana

👁 0 views | ❤️ 0 likes

Your first sales may not come from strangers. They usually come from structure, consistency, and people who already trust you.

Yenkasa Store Team

The first 10 sales are important because they prove your idea can work. They give you confidence, customer feedback, reviews, and money to restock. But many beginners wait for strangers to discover them before using the network they already have.

If you want to know how to get first sales online Ghana, start close, stay consistent, and make buying easy. This guide shows a practical path for fashion sellers.

Start Selling on Yenkasa Store

1. Start with your warm network

Your first buyers may be friends, siblings, coworkers, church members, classmates, or neighbors. Tell them clearly what you sell and ask them to share with people who may need it.

Do not beg. Present your products professionally. A simple message with photos, prices, sizes, and delivery terms can bring your first orders faster than waiting for the algorithm.

2. Use WhatsApp status every day

WhatsApp status is one of the easiest sales tools in Ghana. Post new arrivals, prices, sizes, reviews, delivery updates, and sold items. Let people see that the business is active.

Post at different times: morning, lunch, evening, and weekend. People check status at different times, so one post is not enough.

3. Post consistently on Instagram

Instagram helps people discover your style. Use reels, product photos, customer feedback, and simple captions. Do not hide every price. Make it easy for buyers to understand your offer.

Consistency builds memory. Someone may see your page five times before buying. Keep showing up with useful product information.

4. List on marketplaces early

Do not wait until you are big before using marketplaces. A marketplace can help your products look organized from the beginning. It also gives you links to share when people ask for details.

Yenkasa Store can help beginner sellers move beyond only WhatsApp and Instagram. You can list products and direct interested buyers to a cleaner product page.

5. Ask for reviews after each sale

After delivery, ask the customer if they are happy. If they are, ask for a short review. Share that review on WhatsApp, Instagram, and your store updates.

Your first 10 sales should create proof for the next 20. Do not waste that opportunity.

6. Stay consistent after slow days

Every seller has slow days. Do not disappear because one week is quiet. Improve photos, rewrite captions, repost bestsellers, and follow up politely with interested buyers.

Consistency is what separates a serious seller from someone who only tries for two weeks. Keep showing up, and use every sale to improve the next one.

A simple weekly action plan

Do not treat first sales as something you fix once and forget. Set a simple weekly routine. On Monday, check your stock and update sold items. On Tuesday, improve product descriptions and prices. On Wednesday, post customer proof or delivery updates. On Thursday, share your best listings on WhatsApp status and Instagram stories. On Friday and Saturday, follow up with serious buyers who asked questions but did not complete the order.

This routine is useful for sellers in Accra, Kumasi, Tema, Takoradi, Cape Coast, Tamale, and smaller towns because consistency builds memory. People may not buy the first time they see your product. They may watch for days before trusting you. When your business keeps showing clear information, buyers begin to feel that you are active, reachable, and serious.

Common mistakes to avoid

One common mistake is hiding important details because you want buyers to DM first. That can work sometimes, but many serious buyers are busy. If they cannot see price, size, condition, location, and delivery information quickly, they may move to another seller. Clear information does not reduce sales; it removes confusion and saves your time.

Another mistake is copying what every other seller is doing without checking your own customers. If your buyers care about church wear, office dresses, modest fashion, thrift quality, or quick delivery, build your posts and listings around those needs. Good customer follow-up should make buying easier, not just make your page look busy.

How to turn interest into actual orders

Interest is not the same as money. A like, comment, or “how much?” message is only the beginning. To turn interest into orders, reply clearly, send the right product link, confirm delivery cost early, and make payment instructions simple. Keep your tone polite, but guide the buyer to the next step instead of allowing the conversation to drag for days.
