Why Instagram Sellers in Ghana Are Losing Customers (And How to Fix It)

If people keep liking your posts but not buying, the problem may not be your clothes. It may be the way Instagram forces serious buyers into slow DMs.

Yenkasa Store Team

The painful truth about selling only on Instagram

Instagram can make your fashion business look beautiful. But beauty alone does not close sales. A customer sees a dress, sends a DM, waits for price, asks if it is available, asks for size, asks for delivery, and then disappears.

If you are searching why no sales Instagram business, this is one big reason: buyers get tired before checkout.

"Is this available?" is costing you time

Every Ghana seller knows this question. Sometimes the item is sold. Sometimes the buyer never comes back. Sometimes ten people ask the same question and none of them pay. That back-and-forth drains your energy and makes your business feel unorganized.

Fake buyers waste serious sellers' time

Some people ask for prices, negotiate, request delivery, and vanish. Others say "keep it for me" and never send MoMo. When your whole business depends on DMs, fake buyers can fill your day while real buyers are waiting.

No trust means no sale

Many buyers in Ghana are careful because they have heard stories about wrong sizes, fake pages, delayed delivery, and sellers who stop replying after payment. If your page does not give enough trust, people will admire your product and still refuse to buy.

Instagram vs marketplace: what is the difference?

Instagram is for discovery

Use Instagram to show style, tell your story, post reels, and build attention. It is good for visibility, but it is not always good for organized selling.

A marketplace is for buying

A marketplace gives buyers a clearer shopping path. They see products, prices, descriptions, delivery information, and seller details in one place. That reduces repeated questions and helps serious customers move faster.
